# Wild boar and grey wolf sprite pack

The approved mockups were used with built-in image_gen to create 240 unique poses: 24 frames for each of five authored views, for each animal. Mirroring the three side/diagonal views supplies eight directions. All illustrated poses are generated artwork; packing only isolates and aligns those pixels.

Runtime atlases are in `public/survival/predators-v1/{boar,wolf}/{down,down-left,left,up-left,up}.webp`. Each is 896×1344, four columns by six rows of 224×224 cells. Draw at ground anchor [112,204]. `artwork.json` contains frame names, view/mirror mapping, clip timing, source bounds and registration. `src/predator-art.ts` supplies image keys and timeline helpers; `native/predator-art.ts` supplies an optional Expo offline registry.

Frames 0–3 walk; 4–7 run; 8 idle; 9 blink; 10 alert; 11 anticipation; 12 wind-up; 13 launch; 14 contact; 15 follow-through; 16 attack recovery; 17 ready; 18 hit; 19 hit recovery; 20 stagger; 21 fall; 22 downed; 23 downed eyes closed. The boar has a longer readable wind-up before a tusk-first charge/bash. The wolf crouches then lunges into a bite. Both have an exposed recovery. Timing is specified separately per species in the manifest.

Play sessions seed two boars and two wolves in separate collision-clear areas beyond camp. Attacks commit their direction, respect walls and dodge invulnerability, strike once, and expose recovery. Contact and follow-through poses remain visible even when a close-range attack connects immediately. Red combat bars, hit markers, Dodge and the compact player health meter use the existing combat UI.

Boars have 192 health. Their running charge warns for 0.65 seconds and deals 24 damage with a strong push; their close grounded tusk strike warns for 0.36 seconds and deals 14. A missed charge adds 0.25 seconds to its 0.9-second recovery, rewarding a sidestep. They pursue beyond the old home boundary, catch a walking player, and retain the last seen position for eight seconds. Spaced heavy hits can stagger an early tell, with a two-second stagger cooldown; committed attacks keep their timing and still take full damage. Wolves retain their 144 health, 18-damage lunge, 12-damage bite, 0.5/0.32-second tells and 1.7-second stagger cooldown. They search for nine seconds after losing sight. Both species navigate around obstacles using one shared, bounded path-search budget and require a fresh tell after returning from the bunker.

Boars drop three portions and wolves two. They use the existing red-meat cooking and inventory channel, now displayed as Wild meat alongside deer meat. Serialized venison keys and artwork remain compatible with existing inventory code. Collect, cook, hold, eat and drop work through the current backpack and quick bar. No new recipe or inventory capacity is introduced. Animals are session entities and never alter the editor draft. The animation preview remains available at `/predator-sprites.html`.

Each direction uses a single scale across all 24 frames, alpha-mass horizontal registration and a common foot baseline. Run/launch frames have explicit modest vertical lift. No independent per-frame zoom is used. Boar view corrections in `boar-scale.json` compensate for inconsistent source-sheet packing: the median silhouette mass across ten resting/locomotion frames is matched to the front-diagonal view. This preserves perspective, anatomy and ground anchors while removing the smaller side view; mirrored views share the same correction. Both browser and native rendering call `predatorScale`. The original images and wolf size are preserved. The pack is true alpha with padded silhouettes and lossless WebP. All ten atlases together decode to 48,168,960 bytes. Assets use the existing movement image queue when entering Play, are excluded from editor entry requirements, and are bundled for native offline use. Rendering culls offscreen predators and draws one atlas cell per visible animal. Distant simulation sleeps; AI decisions run at 10 Hz with fixed-step collision movement and bounded pathfinding.

Run `node scripts/prepare-predator-art.mjs` to reproduce extraction/packing from the selected sources. Full source sheets, individual PNG frames, provenance, walk/run/attack GIFs and packing checks are under `docs/qa/predator-sprites-v1`. `generation.json` and `sources/*.json` record the built-in prompts and selected source filenames. Source PNGs are preserved in `source-art`.

After repacking, run `node scripts/calibrate-boar-scale.mjs` to reproduce the view corrections. `check-boar-scale.mjs` independently measures all eight views and renders the actual predator through the native Skia adapter. `check-camp-predators.mjs` covers spawns, shared loot and the full cooking/eating loop. `check-wolf-hunt.mjs` and `check-boar-hunt.mjs` exercise pursuit beyond home, obstacle routing, short/sustained sight loss, actual axe/fist mashing, both attack modes, dodge, sidesteps and death guards. Native renderer checks cover both attacks at all four spawns. A physical iPhone has not been tested.
