# Makeshift bow

Play starts with one bow and 40 arrows in the backpack. Select Bow in the quick selector or equip it from the backpack. Face a target and tap Shoot; hold the action to repeat. The character briefly plants their feet during the draw, then returns to the existing walking or sprinting gait. Standing uses a two-handed ready pose; walking retains the normal gait with a held bow. Five canonical views provide draw, aim, release and recovery poses for all eight directions.

The shot releases at 0.42 seconds, recovers by 0.84 seconds and can repeat every 0.9 seconds. Each release consumes one arrow. Arrows travel at 980 world pixels per second, up to 900 pixels, dealing 34 damage to the first animal or robot hit. Aim assistance stays within 15 degrees of the facing direction. Swept circle collision and four-pixel blocker checks prevent fast arrows passing through small targets or thin walls. Hits use the existing wildlife drops and combat feedback. Misses expire; ammunition can be restocked from the headquarters chest, including bulk transfers.

Five canonical six-frame action atlases serve all eight directions: up-left, left and down-left mirror up-right, right and down-right. The three redundant textures are removed from web and native bundles. The five atlases decode to 16.9 MiB rather than 27 MiB, with 37.5% fewer action textures.

The bow uses the original survivor head, torso, trousers and boots at their original scale. The body keeps its original anchor; the head follows a restrained, continuous draw/recoil/settle translation. Resting sleeves are cut at the shoulders; only isolated arms and equipment from the five action sheets are composited over that core. The side-facing shirt behind the resting sleeve comes from the existing punch artwork, at its original scale. No generated head, torso or legs can replace the character, and there are no stretched body strips or frozen boots over another pair of legs. The original head is painted once without any scaling, rotation or redraw. Its clipping silhouette follows the original hair alpha, rather than a rectangular region that clips nearby bow/arm pixels. The original and moving silhouettes exclude source head paint underneath it. Run `python3 scripts/register-bow-heads.py` to reproduce the silhouette metadata.

Standing with the bow uses a stable two-handed ready pose. Each shot raises, draws, aims, releases and lowers into that same ready pose; repeated fire no longer flashes the walking carry pose between shots. Movement retains the existing walking/sprinting character and held-bow attachment. The nocked arrow disappears at the same release instant that creates the projectile. Five canonical views still serve all eight directions by mirroring.

Artwork source: `bow-and-arrow-eight-direction-assets.zip`, Library ID `libfile_fbf4320628f48191be9b65d2167be077`. Its built-in image generation prompts are included in that pack. Run `python3 scripts/prepare-bow-art.py /path/to/unpacked/assets` to recreate the five runtime textures from the approved PNG frames; then run `python3 scripts/register-bow-layers.py` to reproduce the action registration metadata.

Validation: `npm run typecheck`, `node scripts/check-bow.mjs`, and `node scripts/check-bow-render.mjs`. The render check uses the production Skia renderer to verify the head pixels against the same original sprite translated at unchanged scale across 64 poses, the complete original lower body with equipment hidden, continuous restrained head motion and exact return to rest, uniform equipment scale, the three mirrored pairs, five-texture registry, and identical recovery/ready pixels between shots. `docs/qa/bow-v1/production-poses.png` compares normal, ready, walking, sprinting and every shooting phase. `docs/qa/bow-v1/equipment-only.png` isolates all 30 action layers for inspection.

The legacy `check-backpack.mjs` currently expects three raw chicken and an equipped axe in every new session; the current starter kit has neither. The new bow check uses explicit inventory fixtures for bow, ammo, chest transfers and zero-ammo behavior.
